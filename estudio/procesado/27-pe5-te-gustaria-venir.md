## Qué vas a aprender

- Invitar: *Would you like to come to the match?*
- Ofrecer comida o bebida: *Would you like a burger?*
- Aceptar (*I'd love to*) o rechazar (*Sorry, I can't*).
- Pedir en un bar con *I'd like a coffee, please.*
- Escribir un correo corto de invitación.

## Explicación

*Would you like…?* no es lo mismo que *Do you like…?*

- *Do you like dancing?* = en general, ¿te gusta bailar?
- *Would you like to dance?* = invitación, ¿quieres bailar ahora / conmigo?

**Oferta** (cosa): *Would you like* + nombre. *Would you like a burger? Would you like a coffee?*
Respuesta: *Yes, please. / No, thanks.*

**Invitación** (acción): *Would you like to* + verbo. *Would you like to come to the match? Would you like to see a film?*
Respuesta: *Yes, I'd love to. / Sorry, I can't.*

*I'd like* = *I would like*. Sirve para pedir: *I'd like a burger, please. I'd like a coffee.* También para proponer: *I'd like to ask you something.*

Si no puedes, da un motivo breve: *Sorry, I can't. It's my mum's birthday. / I'm busy on Saturday night.* Luego, si quieres, ofrece otra hora: *But I can meet you there.*

Para quedar: *Let's meet at 3.30 at the entrance. See you there.*

Ritmo: la palabra fuerte cae en *like* y en el nombre o el verbo. Di en voz alta, alargando:

- a **piz**za? → **like** a **piz**za? → Would you **like** a **piz**za?
- **see** a **film**? → to **see** a **film**? → Would you **like** to **see** a **film**?

## Vocabulario

- **Would you like to come?** — ¿Te gustaría venir?
- **I'd love to.** — Me encantaría.
- **Sorry, I can't.** — Lo siento, no puedo.
- **I'd like a coffee, please.** — Querría un café, por favor.
- **How about a coffee?** — ¿Qué tal un café?
- **Let's meet at half past three.** — Quedamos a las tres y media.
- **See you there.** — Nos vemos allí.
- **I don't think it's a good idea.** — No creo que sea buena idea.
- **the entrance** — la entrada
- **the Tube** — el metro de Londres
- **a match** — un partido
- **hungry** — hambriento/a
- **busy** — ocupado/a
- **a party** — una fiesta
- **an exhibition** — una exposición
- **ex-boyfriend** — exnovio

Comida y bebida para ofrecer:

- **a pizza / a salad / a sandwich / a burger** — una pizza / una ensalada / un sándwich / una hamburguesa
- **a coffee / a cola / water / milk and sugar** — un café / un refresco / agua / leche y azúcar

## Frases modelo

Rob invita a Alan al fútbol (completa con: *birthday, 4.00, lunch, match, Sunday, 3.30*):

- Rob: Hey, Alan. Would you like to come to the **match** with me on **Sunday**? It's Norwich against Chelsea, and I have two tickets.
- Alan: Wow! What time's the match?
- Rob: It's at **4.00**.
- Alan: Yeah, I'd love to. Thanks.
- Rob: Would you like to meet for **lunch** first?
- Alan: Sorry, I can't. It's my mum's **birthday**, and I need to have lunch with her. But I can meet you there.
- Rob: Great. Let's meet at **3.30** at the entrance to the Tube station.
- Alan: Fine. See you there.

En el bar del estadio (completa con: *burger, coffee, hungry, know, milk, water*):

- Alan: They are playing really badly. I hope the second half is better.
- Rob: Me too. I **know** they can win. Would you like a **burger**?
- Alan: No, thanks. I'm not very **hungry**.
- Rob: How about a **coffee**?
- Alan: Yeah, great.
- Rob: A burger and a **water**, please.
- Alan: And I'd like a coffee.
- Barman: **Milk** and sugar?
- Alan: Yes, please.

Frases útiles (di en voz alta):

- Would you like to come to the match with me on Sunday?
- Yeah, I'd love to.
- Let's meet at half past three.
- See you there.
- Would you like a burger?
- How about a coffee?
- I'd like to ask you something.
- I don't think it's a good idea.

Invitar a una fiesta:

- A: Would you like to come to a party at my house?
- B: Yes, I'd love to. Thanks! When is it?
- A: It's on Friday at 8.00.
- B: Great! See you there.

Rechazar:

- A: Would you like to come to a party on Saturday?
- B: Sorry, I can't. I'm busy on Saturday night.

Jenny se encuentra con Steve, un ex, en la calle. Lee y elige:

- Steve: Jenny! You look well. Would you like to go to an exhibition this afternoon?
- Jenny: Sorry, I can't. My meeting is in half an hour. It's starting to rain…
- Steve: Would you like to meet one evening?
- Jenny: I don't think it's a good idea.

1. Jenny is looking well / tired.
2. It's raining / starting to rain in the street.
3. Jenny's meeting is in half an hour / an hour.
4. Jenny can / can't go to the exhibition.
5. Jenny thinks it is / isn't a good idea to meet Steve one evening.
