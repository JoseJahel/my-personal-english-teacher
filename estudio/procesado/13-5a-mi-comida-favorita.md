## Qué vas a aprender

- Nombrar comida y bebida (*fish*, *rice*, *coffee*, *orange juice*).
- Usar el presente simple afirmativo y negativo con *I*, *you*, *we*, *they*.
- Hablar de desayuno, comida y cena: *I have cereal for breakfast. I don't eat meat.*
- Decir qué te gusta y qué no: *I like fish. I don't like meat.*

## Explicación

El presente simple con *I*, *you*, *we* y *they* sirve para hábitos (lo que sueles hacer) y para cosas que son verdad: *I have coffee for breakfast. In my country, we eat a lot of rice.*

La forma es la misma para *I*, *you* (singular y plural), *we* y *they*. El verbo no cambia: *I have*, *you have*, *we have*, *they have*.

La negación se hace con *don't* (*do not*). *We don't have coffee.* No *We not have coffee.*

| + | − |
|---|---|
| I have cereal for breakfast. | I don't have eggs for breakfast. |
| You have rice for lunch. | You don't have pasta for lunch. |
| We have coffee for breakfast. | We don't have tea for breakfast. |
| They have fish for dinner. | They don't have meat for dinner. |

Con comida, tres verbos cubren casi todo: *have* (comidas: *have breakfast / lunch / dinner*, o *have cereal*), *eat* (*eat fruit*) y *drink* (*drink tea with milk*).

En un artículo, cuatro personas comentan el desayuno. Nadine desayuna en un café: croissant y café. Pablo desayuna en casa: fruta, yogur y a veces tostadas. Rob no come por la mañana: un café en el trabajo y comida a las 12.30. Sakura desayuna en casa con la familia: arroz, pescado, sopa de miso y té verde.

Lee los comentarios, rodea el *lugar* del desayuno y subraya la comida. Luego di si el desayuno es importante para ti.

La letra *j* siempre es /dʒ/ (*juice*, *vegetables*, *orange*). La *g* a veces es /g/ (*sugar*, *yogurt*, *eggs*) y a veces /dʒ/ (*orange*), sobre todo delante de *e*.

- **chocolate** — chocolate
- **a croissant** — un cruasán

- **miso soup** — sopa de miso

**Bebida**

- **coffee** — café
- **tea / green tea** — té / té verde
- **milk** — leche
- **water** — agua
- **orange juice** — zumo de naranja
- **wine** — vino
- **beer** — cerveza
- **an espresso** — un espresso

**Comidas del día**

- **breakfast** — desayuno (por la mañana)
- **lunch** — comida (al mediodía)
- **dinner** — cena (por la tarde-noche)
- **a meal** — una comida (en general)
- **healthy** — sano/a

**Verbos**

- **have breakfast / lunch / dinner** — desayunar / comer / cenar
- **eat** — comer
- **drink** — beber
- **like / don't like / love** — gustar / no gustar / encantar

Sonidos (di las palabras en voz alta):

- **/dʒ/** (*jazz*): juice, vegetables, orange, Jane
- **/g/** (*girl*): sugar, yogurt, eggs, Grace

## Frases modelo

- We have fruit and cereal for breakfast.
- I don't have breakfast. I have a coffee at work.
- I like fish. I don't like meat.
- I have breakfast at home. What about you?
- I have breakfast at home, too.

Comentarios (léelos en voz alta):

- Nadine, France: I have breakfast in a great café near my office. I have a croissant and coffee – an espresso with hot milk. I love breakfast! It's my favourite meal.
- Pablo, Argentina: I have breakfast at home, but I don't have a big breakfast. I have fruit and yogurt, and sometimes toast. It's a healthy breakfast. That's a good thing at the beginning of the day.
- Rob, UK: I don't eat in the morning – I'm not hungry. I just have a coffee at work. But I have lunch early, at about 12.30.
- Sakura, Japan: I really like breakfast. It's an important meal for Japanese people. I have breakfast at home with my family. We have a traditional breakfast. It isn't very different from lunch and dinner. We have rice, fish, and miso soup and we drink green tea. We don't drink a lot of coffee in my family.

Pronunciación, en voz alta:

- I'm Jane. I like orange juice and vegetables.
- I'm Grace. I have eggs, and coffee with sugar.
