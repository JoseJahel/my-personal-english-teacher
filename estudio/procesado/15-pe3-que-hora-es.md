## Qué vas a aprender

- Preguntar y decir la hora: *What time is it? It's a quarter past three.*
- Usar *o'clock*, *past*, *to*, *quarter* y *half*.
- Decir cómo te sientes: *I'm tired / cold / hungry / hot / thirsty.*
- Frases de prisa y disculpa: *You need to hurry. I'm really sorry.*

## Explicación

*What time is it?* (o *What's the time?*) pide la hora. La respuesta empieza por *It's…*

En punto: *It's three o'clock.* Del 1 al 29 minutos se dice *past* (después de la hora): *five past three*, *ten past three*, *(a) quarter past three* (las 3.15), *twenty past three*, *twenty-five past three*, *half past three* (las 3.30).

A partir de los 31 minutos se mira la hora *siguiente* con *to*: *twenty-five to four*, *twenty to four*, *(a) quarter to four* (las 3.45), *ten to four*, *five to four*.

También puedes decir la hora con números: 7.15 = *(a) quarter past seven* **o** *seven fifteen*. 60 minutes = one hour.

*o'clock* solo en punto. No *It's three fifteen o'clock.*

Saludos por la hora: *Good morning* hasta las 12.00; *Good afternoon* de 12.00 a 18.00; *Good evening* a partir de las 18.00.

Algunas palabras tienen letra muda. En *hour* no suena la *h*; en *what* y *where* tampoco; en *listen* y *Wednesday* hay letras que no se pronuncian.

Cómo te sientes: *I'm tired* (hora de dormir), *I'm cold* (hace 5°), *I'm hungry* (hora de comer), *I'm hot* (hace 35°), *I'm thirsty* (necesito agua).

## Vocabulario

**La hora**

- **What time is it?** — ¿Qué hora es?
- **o'clock** — en punto
- **past** — y (después de la hora)
- **to** — menos (antes de la hora siguiente)
- **(a) quarter** — un cuarto (15 minutos)
- **half past** — y media
- **hour / minute** — hora / minuto
- **a meeting** — una reunión
- **a train** — un tren
- **late** — tarde
- **an hour late** — una hora de retraso

**Cómo te sientes**

- **I'm tired.** — Estoy cansado/a.
- **I'm cold.** — Tengo frío.
- **I'm hungry.** — Tengo hambre.
- **I'm hot.** — Tengo calor.
- **I'm thirsty.** — Tengo sed.
- **Time for lunch. / Time for bed.** — Hora de comer. / Hora de dormir.

**Letras mudas (di en voz alta, sin la letra tachada)**

- **eight** — ocho (*eigh**t***, la *g* no suena)
- **half** — media (*l* muda)
- **hour** — hora (*h* muda)
- **know** — saber (*k* muda)
- **listen** — escuchar (*t* muda)
- **tired** — cansado/a
- **two** — dos (*w* muda)
- **Wednesday** — miércoles (*d* del medio muda)
- **what / where** — qué / dónde (*h* muda)
- **write** — escribir (*w* muda)

*theatre* (UK) = *theater* (US).

Sonido /ɒ/ (*clock*): what, Oxford, sorry, coffee.

## Frases modelo

Horas (di cada una mirando el reloj mental):

- It's three o'clock.
- It's five past three.
- It's ten past three.
- It's (a) quarter past three.
- It's twenty past three.
- It's twenty-five past three.
- It's half past three.
- It's twenty-five to four.
- It's twenty to four.
- It's (a) quarter to four.
- It's ten to four.
- It's five to four.

Rob pregunta la hora:

- Rob: I'm tired. What time is it?
- Alan: It's eleven o'clock.
- Rob: I need to go. I have a meeting in Oxford tomorrow morning.
- Alan: One more drink?
- Rob: Oh, OK!

En la calle:

- Rob: Excuse me. What time is it?
- Woman: It's a quarter to eight. What time's your train?
- Rob: At seven forty-seven.
- Woman: You need to hurry! You only have two minutes.
- Rob: Thanks!

Llega tarde:

- Rob: Hello. I'm Rob Walker. I'm sorry I'm late.
- Man: You're an hour late. It's half past ten.
- Rob: I know. I'm really sorry.

Frases útiles:

- I need to go.
- You need to hurry.
- You're an hour late.
- I'm really sorry.
- Don't worry.
- What a great show!
- It's late and I'm tired.
- Come on.
- OK. Let's go.

Mini diálogo:

- A: What time is it?
- B: It's half past two.
- A: Is the meeting on Wednesday?
- B: I don't know.
