## Qué vas a aprender

- Repasar el presente simple con *I / you / we / they* y con *he / she / it*.
- Repasar comida, frases verbales, trabajos y la hora.
- Leer un texto sobre la dieta japonesa y completar huecos.
- Comprobar si puedes decir tu trabajo, tu desayuno y un día típico.

## Explicación

Este bloque junta 5A, 5B, Inglés práctico 3, 6A y 6B. No hay audio: todo es lectura, escritura y «di en voz alta».

Presente simple:

- *I / you / we / they*: *have / don't have / Do you…? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.*
- *he / she / it*: *works / doesn't work / Does she…? Yes, she does.*
- *have → has, go → goes, do → does.*
- Adverbios: *always, usually, sometimes, never* delante del verbo; *never* no lleva *don't*.
- Hora: *o'clock, past, to, quarter, half.* Pregunta: *What time do you usually have lunch?* (no *What time you have usually lunch?*)

Lee *Eat the Japanese way* y completa con la lista: *coffee, diet, don't, every, good, hamburgers, meat, potatoes, small, stop, vegetables*. La primera está hecha: *diet*.

Autocomprobación al final: si no puedes marcar un *Yes, I can*, vuelve a la lección (5A comida, 6A trabajos, PE3 hora, 6B día típico).

Acento: *break|fast*, *po|ta|toes*, *po|lice o|ffi|cer*, *al|ways*, *u|sual|ly*, *ce|re|al*. La sílaba tónica es la subrayada en el libro: *breakfast*, *potatoes*, *police officer*, *always*, *usually*, *cereal*.

- **go shopping** — ir de compras

- **do housework** — hacer las tareas
- **speak English** — hablar inglés

**Trabajos**

- **a teacher** — un/a profesor/a
- **unemployed** — en paro
- **a waiter** — un camarero
- **retired** — jubilado/a
- **a nurse** — un/a enfermero/a
- **a journalist** — un/a periodista

**Horas (escríbelas en letras)**

- **6.00** — six o'clock
- **10.15** — (a) quarter past ten
- **2.30** — half past two
- **7.45** — (a) quarter to eight
- **4.10** — ten past four
- **5.50** — ten to six

## Frases modelo

Gramática (elige la forma buena y di la frase):

- In Japan, we have rice for breakfast. (no *has*)
- They don't eat meat. (no *not eat*)
- You eat a lot of fast food. (no *eats*)
- I don't drink tea, I prefer coffee.
- Do you want a Coke? (no *Are you want*)
- A: Do they live near here? B: Yes, they do.
- Do you like Mexican food?
- A: What time do we arrive? B: At 5.30.
- Does she speak Spanish?
- He works for a fashion magazine.
- My brother doesn't have children.
- She goes to the gym after work.
- He always has a shower before breakfast. (no *has always*)
- I never go to bed before 12.00. (no *don't never*)
- What time do you usually have lunch?

Eat the Japanese way (texto completo para leer):

- Doctors say that the traditional diet in Japan and other Asian countries is very healthy.
- Why is it good for you? In Japan, people don't eat a lot of red meat, butter, or cheese. They eat a lot of rice and fish and fresh fruit and vegetables. This diet is very good for your heart and people in Japan live longer than in other countries.
- How to eat like the Japanese: Eat rice with your meals and don't eat a lot of potatoes, especially chips. Eat a lot of fish. Don't eat a lot of meat, for example, steak and hamburgers. Eat fresh fruit and vegetables every day. Drink green tea, not coffee. Eat on small plates. Eat slowly. Stop eating when you are full.
