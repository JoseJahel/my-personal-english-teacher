## Qué vas a aprender

- Hablar del fin de semana: *I usually visit my family. We go out on Saturday night.*
- Orden de las preguntas con *be* y con presente simple (*ASI / QuASI*).
- Frases de tiempo libre: *go out, stay at home, meet friends, do housework.*
- Preguntar a otra persona y luego contar *su* fin de semana en tercera persona.

## Explicación

Con *be*, el verbo va delante del sujeto: *They're American.* → *Are they American?* *Where are they from?*

Con otros verbos, el orden es **ASI**: Auxiliary + Subject + Infinitive. *Do you go out on Friday night?*

Si hay palabra interrogativa, es **QuASI**: Question word + Auxiliary + Subject + Infinitive. *Where do you go?* *What music do you like?* *What time does your sister go to bed?*

No mezcles *be* y *do*: *Are you tired on Sunday evening?* (no *Do you tired…?*). *Do you go shopping on Saturday?* (no *Are you go…?*).

Un estudio británico dice que mucha gente hace lo mismo cada sábado y domingo: ver la tele, ir de compras, hacer las tareas. Un 10 % no sale de casa.

Kat es peluquera. Vive en Bristol con su marido, Eddie. Los viernes suelen salir a tomar algo. El sábado hay supermercado, padres, amigos. El domingo se levantan más tarde y ven la tele. *Match of the Day* es un programa británico de fútbol.

Para preguntar el fin de semana de alguien, deja hueco el auxiliar y el sujeto: */ go out on Friday?* → *Do you go out on Friday?* Luego, con otra persona: *Does Nico do sport at the weekend? Yes, he does. He goes to the gym on Saturday morning.*

## Vocabulario

**Tiempo libre**

- **go out (at night)** — salir (por la noche)
- **play computer games / video games** — jugar a videojuegos
- **do sport / do exercise** — hacer deporte / ejercicio
- **go to the beach / the cinema / the theatre** — ir a la playa / al cine / al teatro
- **stay at home** — quedarse en casa
- **play tennis / play the piano** — jugar al tenis / tocar el piano
- **go for a walk (in the mountains)** — dar un paseo (por la montaña)
- **swim** — nadar
- **meet friends** — quedar con amigos
- **relax** — relajarse
- **travel** — viajar
- **go shopping** — ir de compras
- **do housework** — hacer las tareas
- **watch TV / watch sport on TV** — ver la tele / ver deporte
- **visit my family** — visitar a la familia
- **it depends** — depende

**Fin de semana**

- **at the weekend** — el fin de semana
- **Friday / Saturday / Sunday** — viernes / sábado / domingo
- **a hairdresser** — un/a peluquero/a
- **a pub** — un pub
- **per cent (%)** — por ciento

*meet a person for the first time* / *meet at the station* / *meet friends after work.*

Sonidos (di en voz alta):

- **/w/** (*witch*): what, when, why, walk, watch, always
- **/h/** (*house*): how, home, husband, holiday, who
- **/eə/** (*chair*): hairdresser, where, there, their
- **/aʊ/** (*owl*): now, town, out, mountains

## Frases modelo

- What do you do at the weekend? I usually visit my family.
- Do you go out on Friday or Saturday night? Yes, I usually go out with my friends on Saturday night.
- Where do you go? It depends. To the cinema or to a restaurant…
- Does Nico do sport or exercise at the weekend? Yes, he does. He goes to the gym on Saturday morning.

Artículo (lee y comprueba los porcentajes):

- Three out of four British people do the same thing every weekend! After a hard week at work, the weekend is a time to do something fun and exciting. But a new study says that 75% of British people do the same activities every Saturday and Sunday. What do they do? 66% watch TV, 48% go shopping, and 46% do housework. And 10% spend the weekend at home – they never leave the house!

Kat (frases del reportaje; completa los verbos):

- My husband Eddie and I always go out for a drink or for dinner.
- We sometimes meet friends there, too.
- In the evening we usually stay at home and watch TV.

Ritmo:

- A: What do you do at the weekend? B: I walk, or I watch TV.
- A: Who's Henry? B: He's my husband.
- A: Where do they live? B: Their house is over there.
- A: How do you relax? B: I go to the mountains near my town.
