## Qué vas a aprender

- Nombrar objetos pequeños: teléfono, llaves, paraguas, cartera…
- Usar *a / an* y el plural regular (*bags, classes, countries*).
- Preguntar *What is it?* / *What are they?*
- Decir qué llevas en el bolso o el bolsillo.

## Explicación

*What is it?* (no *What's it?*) identifica un objeto. Respuesta: *It's a book. It's an umbrella.*

Usa *a* delante de consonante: *a bag, a phone, a key*. Usa *an* delante de sonido vocálico: *an umbrella, an ID card*. Cuenta el sonido, no la letra: *an hour* llevaría *an* por la *h* muda; aquí *an ID* porque *I* suena /aɪ/.

En plural, *be* cambia a *are*: *They're books. They're keys.*

Reglas de ortografía del plural:

| | Singular | Plural | Regla |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a bag, a holiday | bags, holidays | añadir *-s* |
| 2 | a class | classes /ɪz/ | *-es* tras *ch, sh, s, ss, x* |
| 3 | a country | countries | consonante + *y* → *-ies* |

*the* va con singular o plural ya conocido: *Look at the board. Open the door. Close the windows.*

Tres sonidos de plural: /z/ (*bags, phones, keys, pens*), /s/ (*books, coats, passports, tablets*), /ɪz/ (*watches, glasses, pieces, purses*).

El texto impreso *Oh no! Where's my phone?* lista ocho cosas que la gente busca: *pens and pencils; glasses and sunglasses; keys; wallets and purses; bank cards; mobile phones; umbrellas; phone chargers*. Ordena la lista según tu vida, no según una pista.

## Vocabulario

- **a (mobile) phone** — un móvil
- **a watch** — un reloj
- **a tablet** — una tableta
- **a bag** — una bolsa
- **a wallet** — una cartera (de hombre, típicamente)
- **a purse** — un monedero
- **a pencil** — un lápiz
- **a notebook** — un cuaderno
- **glasses / sunglasses** — gafas / gafas de sol
- **a photo** — una foto
- **an ID card** — un DNI / carné
- **a passport** — un pasaporte
- **a camera** — una cámara
- **a bank card** — una tarjeta bancaria
- **a (phone) charger** — un cargador
- **a key / keys** — una llave / llaves
- **an umbrella** — un paraguas
- **a book / a laptop** — un libro / un portátil
- **What is it? It's a…** — ¿Qué es? Es un/a…
- **What are they? They're…** — ¿Qué son? Son…

*ph* se lee /f/: *phone, photo*.

Sonido /z/ (*zebra*): *zero, Brazil, is, he's*.

## Frases modelo

- Is it an ID card? No, it's a bank card.
- What is it? It's a book. / It's a key. / It's an umbrella. / It's an ID card.
- What are they? They're books. / They're keys. / They're watches. / They're dictionaries.
- For me, number one is my glasses.
- In my bag, I have a book, keys, a pen…
- What's… in English? How do you spell it?

Texto impreso:

- Every day people all over the world say, 'Oh no! Where's my…?' The top eight things that people look for are: pens and pencils; glasses and sunglasses; keys (house keys and car keys); wallets and purses; bank cards; mobile phones; umbrellas; phone chargers.

Fotos de situaciones (describe el objeto que encaja):

- recepción de hotel → *a passport* o *an ID card*
- control de seguridad → *a laptop*, *a bag*
- avión → *a phone*, *a book*
- recepción → *a key*, *a bag*
- taxi → *a wallet*, *a phone*
